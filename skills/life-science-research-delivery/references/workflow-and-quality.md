# Workflow and quality reference

This reference turns the project's P1–P9 research pipeline into portable Skill instructions. The stages are a method, not evidence that a given tool or integration is available. Follow only stages supported by the user's request and available sources/tools.

## P1 — Confirm intent and scope

Identify the research object, deliverable, audience, and what the reader should understand. Classify the topic as focused, broad, ambiguous, or out of scope. For a broad topic that yields multiple distinct reviews, present 2–4 clearly differentiated directions and wait for the user to choose. State minimum assumptions and exclusions. Do not search or assert research findings here.

## P2 — Design the research story and plan

Create a topic-specific narrative with 3–6 major modules, their purpose, order, and transitions. Then derive 3–6 internal research questions, bilingual search concepts, evidence needs, and stopping conditions. The internal questions are not automatically report headings. Preserve the user's selected scope; don't force a hypothesis/validation template onto descriptive, technical, comparative, or translational topics.

## P3 — Screen actual candidates

Screen only retrieved candidate records. For each, record keep/reserve/drop, relevant research question, intended use, reason, priority, evidence type, and flags. Do not add records, infer findings from titles, or retain weak sources just to reach a quota. Deduplicate the same underlying paper across providers.

## P4 — Extract traceable evidence

For every retained source, extract the exact study object/model, design, conditions, comparator, result, units, follow-up, limitations, and access level. Attach stable evidence IDs and locators (page/section/table/figure when available). Label abstract-only evidence. If a needed detail is absent, record it as unknown.

## P5 — Draft the research report

Write to the agreed audience and depth, following the topic-specific story. Put citations near factual claims. Separate source findings, synthesis, and interpretation. Include methods/scope and limitations. Do not pad length or turn a proposed mechanism into an established fact.

## P6 — Build the slide content pack

Design an academic narrative, not a mechanical split of the report. Each slide has one communication purpose, a title, concise content preserving important values and comparison conditions, supporting evidence, visual plan, and speaker notes that can be spoken naturally. Keep claims aligned with the report. Recommend source figures only with a locator; otherwise specify an original diagram and do not imply image reuse rights.

## P7 — Atomize claims for checking

Extract the smallest independently checkable factual claims from the report and slides, prioritizing numbers, comparisons, causal language, trends, efficacy, safety, approvals, and development stage. Link each claim to its citation/evidence IDs and location. Exclude headings, scope notes, and repeated paraphrases.

## P8 — Verify claims against records

Check the cited records for the correct subject, predicate, direction, number, unit, conditions, comparator, time point, research stage, and strength of language. Mark supported, partly supported, unsupported, or not determinable. Apply an explicit action: retain, narrow/rewrite, remove, or move to unresolved questions. Do not substitute general knowledge for missing source evidence.

## P9 — Assemble and inspect final deliverables

Apply verification actions to both report and slides, reconcile citations and terminology, and inspect the final rendered/exported text. Check for unsupported claims, mismatched values, dangling references, broken figures, empty/redundant slides, malformed punctuation, and internal audit labels. Keep unresolved issues visible in an appendix. Human domain review remains necessary; a schema/test pass is not scientific approval.

## Evidence and output checklist

- [ ] User-confirmed topic and scope; no silently chosen direction.
- [ ] Search sources, query date, and access level are disclosed.
- [ ] Every material factual claim maps to a real source and locator where available.
- [ ] Evidence is not overstated across models, populations, time horizons, or study types.
- [ ] Conflicting and negative evidence is represented when relevant.
- [ ] Report and slides have the same scope, values, and evidence strength.
- [ ] Important claims have explicit verification status and action.
- [ ] Figure reuse/licensing is not implied by a link.
- [ ] Deliverable clearly states what was and was not verified.
- [ ] Tool availability was checked in this session; each source stream actually used is named, and unavailable streams are disclosed.
- [ ] Scientific literature and patents are kept as distinct evidence classes; patent records are not treated as proof of efficacy or safety.
- [ ] No credentials, private user data, or unsupported claim of API/tool execution appears in deliverables.
- [ ] The topic is within the Skill's life-science scope, and patient-specific clinical decisions are not presented as research assistance.

## Known demonstration boundary

The project has a documented end-to-end local validation for “chronic implant neural interfaces,” but its own validation record cautions that long-term direct evidence was thin, human editorial review affected a readable sample, and figure reuse permissions were unknown. Treat this as a case study and workflow demonstration, not proof that every topic or a public hosted endpoint is fully validated. See the bundled example summary.
